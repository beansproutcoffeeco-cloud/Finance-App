# 17hats: User Feedback, Weaknesses & Opportunities

> Research agent #12 of 12 — user sentiment, complaints, churn drivers, and the opportunity map for a competing product.
> Compiled 2026-07-07. Sources: G2, Capterra, GetApp, TrustRadius, Trustpilot, Software Advice, JustUseApp (App Store review aggregator), photography forums (DPReview, FredMiranda, PhotographyTalk), practitioner blogs, and competitor "17hats alternatives" content.
>
> **Methodology note & caveats:** G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, the App Store, and Google Play block direct page scraping, so individual review text below was recovered via search-index excerpts of those pages plus third-party aggregations. Quotes are reproduced as found and attributed to the platform where they appear; exact reviewer names/dates were not always recoverable, and dates are given where known. A meaningful share of "17hats weaknesses" content on the web is written by competitors (Plutio, OneSuite, Taskip, Bloom, HoneyBook, Zendo, SwellSystem, AgencyHandy) — those claims are flagged as competitor-sourced and were cross-checked against user reviews where possible. Do not treat competitor claims as neutral. Nothing below is fabricated; where evidence was thin, that is stated.

## 1. Summary

17hats is a **well-liked but aging product**. Across ~275 reviews on the major B2B review sites it averages roughly **4.4–4.6 / 5**, and its core users — solo photographers, wedding pros, home stagers, planners, coaches, dog trainers — are unusually loyal ("been with 17hats since it started… would not use any other software"). What they love is consistent and specific: **all-in-one consolidation** (CRM + quotes + contracts + e-sign + invoicing + scheduling + questionnaires + basic bookkeeping), **workflow automation that actually saves hours**, **templates for everything**, the **quote → contract → invoice combined document**, and **fast, human chat support**.

The complaint list is equally consistent and has barely changed since ~2019, which is itself the biggest signal: **dated UI ("stuck in 2015")**, a **weak, glitchy mobile app** (3.3/5 on JustUseApp's App Store aggregate; "basically useless," "crashes every time I open it"), **shallow bookkeeping and near-zero reporting/analytics**, **limited team/multi-user capability**, a **steep learning curve with an outdated onboarding experience**, **slow development pace** ("2,700 feature requests per year… nothing we can really do"), **nickel-and-dime add-ons** ($5/mo each for bank connect, time tracking, extra users, extra brands; SMS number leasing; ~$85/mo real-world cost vs. the "$60 all-inclusive" headline), and **hard data lockout at cancellation**.

Churn flows in both directions: design-conscious and team-scaling users leave for **HoneyBook** (modern UX, smart files) or **Dubsado** (deep customization, visual flow builder), and photographers who want industry depth go to **Tave / Sprout Studio / Bloom**; meanwhile HoneyBook's **~89% price hike (Feb 2025)** and Dubsado's setup burden push users *toward* 17hats. The opportunity for a new product is not "more features" — it's **modern UX + genuinely good mobile + real financial reporting + team support + transparent pricing**, wrapped around the same automation core 17hats users already prove they'll pay for.

## 3. What users love (ranked themes)

### 3.1 All-in-one consolidation — the #1 theme by far
The core promise ("stop juggling 5 tools") is the thing reviewers cite first, and it is why they stay for 5–10 years.

- "The lead-to-quote-to-invoice-to-project workflows with everything you and a small team needs, all in 1 place, is invaluable… a lot of features for a very affordable price." — TrustRadius review
- "The seamless integration of quotes, contracts, invoices, and automated workflows has completely transformed how I run my business. It saves time, eliminates back-and-forth emails, and gives clients a smooth, professional experience." — TrustRadius review
- The annual plan "is cheaper than all the different solutions combined" that the business used before, and "because everything is under one platform, it saves time from going app to app." — Staged4more (home-staging blog, long-term user review)
- "Has been with 17hats since it started… would not use any other software"; "using 17hats for nearly 7 years… intuitive to use, with great team support"; "using 17hats since 2018… couldn't do without it." — long-tenure reviewers surfaced via Capterra/blog reviews
- You can run **multiple businesses/brands under one account** and keep the accounting separate — praised on G2.

### 3.2 Workflow automation that pays for itself
- Automation of "workflows, questionnaires, invoices, reminders, and online scheduling allows me to handle more work with less effort." — G2 review summary
- "Automation saved me valuable time while reducing errors and ensuring consistent communication with clients" — recurring sentiment in 17hats' user community and reviews.
- 17hats can auto-trigger a workflow based on a lead's form answers — cited by an online business manager as the concrete reason 17hats beats HoneyBook: HoneyBook "forces you to manually intervene in the workflow process." — Christi Johnson Creative (2023–24 comparison post)
- If/then conditional questionnaires (branching questions) called out as a differentiator vs. other photo CRMs. — AdventureInstead "Best CRMs for Photographers" (2025)

### 3.3 Templates + combined Quote/Contract/Invoice documents
- Users "LOVE the templates feature — everything from emails, quotes, invoices, and contracts" is a customizable template. — user review (Capterra/blog corpus)
- "Create a custom quote, contract and invoice within 10 minutes of a client call… quote, contract and invoicing is 17hats' strength." — Spruce Rd. (designer blog review)
- The **optional add-ons on quotes** (client can self-upgrade packages) "is worth the investment alone, and has deterred me from switching to alternative software." — user review
- Combining Quote + Contract + Invoice into one document "saved time, reduced errors, and ensured consistent high-quality communication for every client." — 17hats user community

### 3.4 Payments & invoicing flexibility (client-facing)
- Clients can pay online via Stripe/Square/PayPal/Authorize.net; split payments ("50% now, 50% on due date"), payment schedules, and auto payment reminders repeatedly praised. — Staged4more, Capterra reviews
- Recurring invoices/billing exists and works (weekly/monthly/annual).

### 3.5 Customer support (majority-positive)
- "17hats customer support is responsive, helpful, and resolves issues in a timely and respectful manner." — G2 review summary
- "You can talk with real people in customer support online, and they answer within minutes." — Capterra review
- Note: this is contested — see §4.7 for the minority negative view. Support is chat/email only, Mon–Fri 7am–4pm PT, no phone (confirmed by 17hats' own help center).

### 3.6 Email sync tied to clients/projects
- Client emails auto-sync (Gmail etc.) and get "tracked and filed into different projects." — Staged4more. This CRM-inbox linkage is a retention feature users mention losing when they consider leaving.

### 3.7 Fast time-to-value (relative to Dubsado)
- G2 summary: most users implement in under a month, ROI within ~4 months. Compare Dubsado's commonly-reported **20–40 hours** of initial configuration. 17hats is the "simple admin" choice in nearly every comparison article.

### 3.8 Newer additions users like (2024–2026)
- **Two-way SMS texting** tied to clients/projects and scheduling reminders (added ~2025; $60 one-time setup + monthly number lease — praised as a feature, dinged as an upcharge).
- **14 visual pipelines** ("visual command center").
- **Free CRM tier** (unlimited contacts/projects, 4 invoices/quarter) as an on-ramp — though reviewers note 4 invoices/quarter "makes it unusable for active professionals" (PhotoCRM.io, 2026).

## 4. What users complain about (ranked themes, with recency)

### 4.1 Dated UI / design — chronic, still current (2015 → 2026)
The single most repeated criticism, present in reviews from every era and **still cited in 2025–2026 content**.

- "The interface looks stuck in 2015… every client-facing document reflects that dated design." — SwellSystem comparison (2026, competitor-sourced but matching user reviews)
- "Dated UI. 17hats works, but it won't impress design-conscious clients." — G2 review summary (2025–26)
- "If anything, there are moments where I wish the interface were just a touch more modern or streamlined." — G2 review (2024–25)
- "Not very well-designed… you have to search around to find things, and some ways the interface is set up just don't make sense." — Capterra review
- "The design isn't the prettiest… the calendar view never worked quite the way I wanted it to." — TrustRadius review
- Trustpilot: the contract/questionnaire **editor is outdated** — "constant issues in text selection and the undo feature barely working." (recent)
- Historical: a user who tried 17hats in 2015/2016 called it "horrendous… confusing to use as a CRM newbie, ugly UI back then" (designbylaney.com, 2025 retrospective). The complaint has softened but never disappeared.
- No CSS customization of documents or the client portal; portal branding only partially overridable (logo/colors). — Plutio/Prospeo comparisons, 2026.

### 4.2 Mobile app is weak — chronic AND current; worst-rated surface
- Aggregate app rating **3.3/5** (JustUseApp, 31+ reviews, 2025).
- "Won't even launch… simply crashes every time I open it." — App Store review
- "17hats has shown a blank screen for 2–3 months now." — App Store review
- "Contacts stuck on 'Loading All Contacts'… projects not responding when tapped… I can add clients but not projects — basically useless." — App Store reviews (via JustUseApp)
- "The calendar doesn't actually show events or appointments for anything other than the current month and it freezes up entirely when trying to access documents within a workflow." — App Store review
- "Stop focusing on spiffing up the login page & UI beautification and focus on functionality." — App Store review
- Accepting bookings on the phone app vs. laptop caused clients to "get every correspondence double time, for example 2 of the same invoices — extremely stressful to clients." — App Store review
- Have to log in every time; no Face ID; no offline mode. — multiple app reviews
- "The app version needs to be updated. The mobile [web] version offers several features that the app is not set up yet to tackle." — G2/Capterra review
- Recency: complaints span 2019 → 2025; the app remains far behind the web product. Feature gap between mobile web and native app is itself a complaint.

### 4.3 Shallow bookkeeping & near-zero reporting/analytics — chronic, current
- "The only area where 17Hats falls short is in its bookkeeping features. While it offers basic financial tracking, it lacks advanced accounting tools found in dedicated software." — TrustRadius review
- "There's little to nothing on reporting or analytics." — Capterra/TrustRadius review (repeated verbatim across platforms)
- Reporting is basically P&L + sales-tax + lead-source; no customizable reports. "I sometimes wish there were even more customization options in templates and **reporting**." — G2 review (2024–25)
- **QuickBooks sync problems** are a named churn driver: "one user left after significant QuickBooks sync errors that created double payment records." — review corpus (Trustpilot/Capterra). Known sync limitations: expenses don't sync to QBO; US/CA/UK only; invoice ref numbers ≤21 chars.
- "The bookkeeping function isn't always the easiest to navigate." — Capterra review
- Bank Connect (auto expense import) costs **$5/mo extra**, which users find galling inside an "all-inclusive" plan.
- No multi-currency support found anywhere in docs or reviews (gap confirmed by absence).

### 4.4 Learning curve & dated onboarding — chronic, softened but current
- "A steep learning curve is the consensus complaint across review platforms… the 7-day trial barely scratches the surface." — Prospeo review roundup (2026)
- "With the large amount that 17hats offers, there are a lot of details to learn, so it can take a little while to get up and running." — Capterra review
- "Set up is quite time consuming." — G2 review
- Onboarding UX: "it doesn't catch your eye — it's simply a part of the dashboard, not highlighted or differentiated in any way, so you can easily miss it." — Zendo teardown (2025; competitor-sourced but a concrete, checkable claim)
- Help-center docs described as outdated: "difficult to figure out what features you have access to due to outdated documentation." — AdventureInstead (2025)
- Counterpoint: a real minority say "the learning curve was very easy" — the split appears to track tech-comfort of the buyer.

### 4.5 Pricing structure & nickel-and-diming — NEW/intensified 2025–2026
- 2025: 17hats collapsed 3 tiers into **one "all-inclusive" plan** — $60/mo, $600/yr, $800/2yr (50% first-term promos common).
- "The 'all-inclusive' positioning feels misleading when bank connect, time tracking, and recurring billing each cost extra." — Taskip pricing teardown (2026, competitor-sourced; add-on prices confirmed on 17hats.com: $5/mo each for Bank Connect, Time Tracking, extra user beyond 3, extra brand; SMS = $60 setup + monthly number lease)
- "A solo user who adds bank connect, time tracking, recurring billing, and QuickBooks is looking at approximately **$85/month** on the monthly plan." — Taskip/AgencyHandy pricing analyses (2026)
- 17hats' own pricing page still shows a "levels" grid with document limits (20/35/unlimited per month) that "looks exactly like tiered plans even though the headline is one all-inclusive plan" — flagged as confusing. — Taskip (2026)
- "No cheaper or limited version for beginners… if you only need invoicing or CRM, you still have to pay the full price." — OneSuite pricing guide (2026, competitor-sourced)
- Affordability churn (pre-dates repricing too): "There were no issues with the software at all. We just were not in the position to keep paying the amount that was required." — Capterra churned-user review
- "Its pricing is a bit high — especially for solopreneurs." — review roundup (2025)

### 4.6 Slow development velocity — chronic, current, and corrosive
- "When I contact support about issues, I'm told they get **2,700 feature requests per year** and often told 'there's nothing we can really do' or that developers were working on it." — Trustpilot review (recent)
- "Users criticized the slow pace of development and called the mobile app poorly designed." — Trustpilot/Capterra corpus
- "HoneyBook is taking the lead because they have more frequent updates." — G2/Capterra review
- "Relatively new with occasional glitches that can get frustrating" (older, ~2016–2018) → "apps either work or don't work, with glitches intense enough to no longer rely on it" (more recent). Stability complaints persist at low volume.

### 4.7 Support access — minority complaint, current
- No phone support ever (company policy, stated in help center); chat/email Mon–Fri 7am–4pm PT only.
- "A small group of reviewers report limited support channels, slow response times, and difficulty accessing hands-on assistance." — GetApp review summary
- "When a platform accessibility issue occurred, the customer support response was on the slow side." — Capterra review
- Weekend/evening businesses (wedding shooters work Saturdays) notice the coverage gap.

### 4.8 Workflow/automation ceilings — chronic, current
- Workflows support only three step types ("a to-do item, an action item, and a pause item") — "may not be robust enough for those prioritizing automation." — AdventureInstead (2025)
- **No automatic reminder when a client hasn't completed a questionnaire**: "you have to look and see whether or not they completed it and manually trigger that email… there's no way for the system to see that it hasn't been completed to send it automatically." — Solution Integrators comparison (2025)
- "Base date versus project date is confusing, and I'm constantly having to go in and out of workflows and emails to make changes." — Capterra review
- No node/visual builder (vs. Dubsado Flows 3.0); no CSS on forms; limited conditional logic vs. Dubsado.

### 4.9 Scheduling & calendar rough edges — chronic
- "The calendar view doesn't work as desired… scheduling features aren't very robust." — Capterra/TrustRadius reviews
- "There's no option for people to book calls through the platform" — older Capterra complaint (online scheduling has since shipped and improved; recent complaints center on availability logic: Google events marked "Free" still block 17hats booking slots — confirmed in 17hats' own help docs).
- 17hats' recommended workaround for reliable availability is maintaining **5 separate calendars** — a signal of underlying design complexity.

### 4.10 Payments configuration rigidity — chronic
- "You can implement Stripe or PayPal, but not both active at the same time" for a client's invoice; payment methods are **global, not per-client/per-invoice** — "if you disable credit cards site-wide, you must change payment methods for everyone." — Capterra reviews

### 4.11 Team/multi-user limitations — chronic, increasingly cited as users grow
- Plan includes 3 users; extra seats $5/mo. Permission model is coarse (Owner / Admin / Team Member grants).
- "If you have a team, you might still need separate project management software — 17hats isn't very sophisticated when it comes to assigning tasks and working as a team." — Capterra review
- Older reviews: "only one user is allowed per account" (pre-multi-user era, pre-2020 — now outdated but shows the lineage).
- No Kanban boards, no Gantt, no task dependencies, no internal chat, no role-based granularity. — Plutio/OneSuite/SwellSystem comparisons (2026, competitor-sourced; Kanban/Gantt absence is verifiable).
- Competitor phrasing "no team features at all" **overstates** it — multi-user exists — but "not built for teams" is a fair summary of user sentiment.

### 4.12 Data lockout at cancellation — current, trust-damaging
- "Once your subscription ends, you lose access to your data, including contracts, client information, and invoices. Client and financial records become inaccessible immediately unless exported in advance." — review corpus (Subscribed.fyi / Capterra). A recurring anxiety and a switching-cost complaint that also deters signups.

### 4.13 Email deliverability — real but mostly self-inflicted / infrastructural
- No large volume of "my emails go to spam" reviews was recoverable, but 17hats maintains an entire help-center category on deliverability, recommends mail-tester.com checks, warns that sending via the 17hats mail server **from a free @gmail/@yahoo address makes mail "much, much more likely to be classified as spam and rejected,"** and pushes users to configure SPF/DKIM/DMARC themselves. Occasional user reports of email sync disconnects exist. Interpretation: deliverability is a known operational footgun the product pushes onto the user — an opportunity for a competitor to handle domain auth automatically.

### 4.14 Niche-depth gaps by vertical — chronic
- Home staging: "not a staging operations tool — no inventory management, furniture tracking, load lists." — Staged4more
- Photography: no galleries/proofing (Bloom, Sprout Studio have them), less photo-industry premade data than Tave. — Bloom/Zendo comparisons
- Email filing edge case: when a client mentions another project in the same email thread, it files under the wrong project. — Staged4more

## 5. Why people leave / choose alternatives (by segment)

### 5.1 → HoneyBook (design-conscious creatives, mobile-first freelancers)
**Why they go:** modern UI and client-facing polish ("minimalistic design and smooth interface" vs. 17hats' dated look); "smart files" (interactive proposal+contract+invoice+payment in one branded doc); better mobile app and messaging; faster release cadence ("HoneyBook is taking the lead because they have more frequent updates" — 17hats reviewer); simpler flat 3% payments story.
**Why the flow reversed in 2025:** HoneyBook's Feb 4, 2025 repricing — Starter +89.5% ($19→$36), Essentials +51% ($39→$59), Premium +63% ($79→$129) — "has photographers shopping around" (PhotoCRM.io, Maroo, Taskip, 2025–26). 17hats at ~$50–60/mo all-features with 3 users became the value option.
**Who comes back to 17hats from HoneyBook:** users who found HoneyBook's threaded Activity Feed inbox "confusing for clients… difficulty combing through countless messages," clients hitting verification-code friction on every file, clients only using the portal for payments, and automation gated behind higher tiers requiring manual intervention. (Danielle Guillermo blog; Christi Johnson Creative.)

### 5.2 → Dubsado (systems nerds, OBMs, customization power users)
**Why they go:** far deeper customization — CSS on forms, conditional logic, visual node-based Flows (3.0), multi-step automations triggered by contract signing/payment/status; more control over client portal branding. 17hats' 3-step workflow model and missing auto-reminders (e.g., un-completed questionnaire) feel like a ceiling.
**Friction that keeps people at 17hats:** Dubsado's 20–40 hour setup, automation locked to the Premier plan ($55/mo), weak mobile app, "no role-based access control, can't edit invoices without recreating them, limited financial tracking, occasional email disconnects" (Reddit complaints summarized in Plutio's 2026 comparison).

### 5.3 → Tave / Sprout Studio / Bloom / Pixifi (photographers wanting industry depth)
**Why they go:** Tave's photography-specific premade workflows, industry data, deeper automations ("its workflow features are above all the others" — PhotographyTalk/DPReview forum users); Tave improved onboarding while "17hats hasn't introduced a more modern onboarding experience" (Zendo, 2025). Bloom/Sprout add client galleries & proofing that 17hats lacks entirely. Forum sentiment (DPReview, FredMiranda): 17hats = easier to learn and migrate to; Tave = more powerful for a serious studio.

### 5.4 → QuickBooks/Xero + a lighter CRM (finance-first users)
**Why they go:** bookkeeping shallowness and QBO sync errors ("double payment records"); no real accounting (no balance sheet depth, no accountant access, expenses don't sync); reporting limited to P&L/sales tax. These users keep a real ledger elsewhere and then question paying $60/mo for the rest.

### 5.5 → Cheaper/free all-in-ones (price-sensitive solopreneurs; agencies outgrowing it)
**Why they go:** "$60/mo + add-ons" total cost; no budget tier since the 2025 single-plan change; teams outgrow the 3-seat, no-Kanban, coarse-permissions model. Destinations cited across 2025–26 alternative roundups: Bloom, Plutio, Agiled, OneSuite, Taskip, Zendo, ManyRequests, Flowlu, Maroo (wedding vendors). One representative churn quote: "There were no issues with the software at all. We just were not in the position to keep paying the amount that was required."

### 5.6 Dealbreakers by segment (condensed)
| Segment | Dealbreaker(s) |
|---|---|
| Wedding/portrait photographers | No galleries/proofing; weaker photo-industry templates than Tave; Saturday support gap |
| Design-conscious brands | Dated client-facing documents; no CSS/branding control; portal carries 17hats look |
| Growing studios/agencies (3+ people) | Coarse permissions, no Kanban/Gantt/dependencies, per-seat add-on cost |
| Finance-rigorous owners | Shallow bookkeeping, QBO sync errors, no analytics, no multi-currency |

| Budget solopreneurs | Single $60 plan + $5 add-on stack; free tier capped at 4 invoices/quarter |
| International users | US-centric: payments, QBO sync (US/CA/UK), no multi-currency |

## 6. Recurring feature requests

Collected across reviews, forums, and comparison content; ordered by frequency/recency of mention.

1. **Modern UI refresh** — including the document/contract editor (text selection and undo are broken per Trustpilot) and client-facing docs.
2. **Real mobile app parity** — full feature set, stability, Face ID / stay-logged-in, offline access, reliable calendar beyond current month.
3. **Reporting & analytics** — custom reports, sales pipeline analytics, booking-rate/conversion metrics, revenue forecasting (today: only P&L, sales tax, lead source).
4. **Deeper bookkeeping** — expense sync to QBO (or built-in accounting good enough to skip QBO), reliable payment sync (no duplicates), multi-currency, accountant access.
5. **Smarter automation** — condition-aware triggers (e.g., auto-remind when questionnaire NOT completed), more step types than to-do/action/pause, visual workflow builder, workflow-level branching.
6. **Client portal & document branding control** — custom CSS/themes, white-labeling, less 17hats branding.
7. **Team features** — granular roles/permissions, task assignment views (Kanban), internal notes/chat, activity logs.
8. **Per-invoice/per-client payment method control** — allow Stripe AND PayPal simultaneously; per-document processor choice.
9. **More customization in templates** ("even more customization options in templates and reporting" — G2).
10. **Calendar/scheduling robustness** — respect Google "Free/Busy" status, fewer required workaround calendars, richer booking rules (some shipped 2024–25: Availability Rules, Calendar Checks — requests continue).
11. **Included (not add-on) time tracking, bank feeds, and recurring billing** — the $5 add-on stack is the sore point.
12. **Data access after cancellation** — export-everything and/or read-only grace mode.
13. **Bundled email domain authentication** — automatic SPF/DKIM/DMARC setup instead of DIY deliverability homework.
14. **Vertical depth** — galleries/proofing (photogs), inventory (stagers), event-day logistics (planners).

## 7. Opportunity map for a new competing app (ranked)

Framing: 17hats' users are proof of demand for an **automation-first, all-in-one back office for solo service pros at ~$30–60/mo**. The wedge is everything users tolerate, not love.

| # | Gap | Evidence | How we'd win |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Modern, design-forward UX (incl. client-facing docs)** | "Stuck in 2015"; "won't impress design-conscious clients"; editor undo broken; #1 reason for HoneyBook defections | Ship a genuinely beautiful doc/proposal experience (HoneyBook-class smart files) on top of 17hats-class automation. Client-visible surfaces are the marketing: every proposal a prospect receives is a demo. Offer themes + CSS/brand tokens — the thing neither 17hats (no CSS) nor HoneyBook (limited) fully gives. |

| 9 | **Support coverage matching the customer's calendar** | Chat/email only, M–F 7am–4pm PT; wedding pros work weekends; minority complaints about slow responses; "2,700 feature requests… nothing we can do" | Weekend chat coverage + public roadmap/changelog with visible shipped-request counts. Slow, opaque development is a stated defection reason — velocity itself is a marketable differentiator. |
| 10 | **Payment flexibility** | Global-only processor settings; Stripe XOR PayPal; SMS costs extra | Per-invoice payment method selection, multiple processors live simultaneously, included SMS reminders (cost is trivial; charging for it reads as a nickel). |
| 11 | **Vertical depth modules** | Stagers need inventory; photogs need galleries; planners need event logistics; Tave wins on industry data | Core platform + optional vertical packs (template data, industry workflows, gallery/inventory add-ons). 17hats is deliberately horizontal ("17 hats"); the counter-position is "feels custom-built for your industry in 10 minutes." |
| 12 | **Calendar sanity** | "5 calendars" workaround; Free-status events block bookings; double-booking anxieties | Single availability engine reading real free/busy across Google/Outlook/iCloud, buffer/travel-time rules, no workaround calendars. Table stakes done right beats features. |

**Positioning synthesis:** the market has an *automation leader with bad UX* (Dubsado), a *UX leader with expensive, shallower automation* (HoneyBook), and a *value all-rounder with 2015 bones* (17hats). No one currently holds: **modern UX + deep-but-easy automation + real finances + real mobile + honest pricing**. That intersection is empty, and 2025's category-wide price hikes created an unusually switch-willing customer base.
